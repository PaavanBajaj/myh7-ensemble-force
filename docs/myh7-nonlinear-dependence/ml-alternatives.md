# MYH7 high-dimensional relationships: predictive ML alternatives and decision

Research date: 2026-10-02. This note is an independent primary-source method review grounded in the existing private and public MYH7 work. Recommendations below are judgments about this particular dataset, not a published benchmark demonstrating a universally best method.

## Definite method recommendation

Implement a **distance-correlation exploratory extension** to the frozen Spearman screen, with pairwise scores and a small set of prespecified multivariate feature blocks against each outcome. Include the usual nonnegative sample distance correlation for easy reading and the normalized U-centered, bias-corrected squared distance-correlation score for small-sample sensitivity. Preserve negative bias-corrected scores. Add study/variant deletion checks and a label-blind scaling sensitivity. This directly addresses whether multivariate structural or sequence differences track outcome differences without training another highly flexible predictor. Distance correlation applies to random vectors of different dimensions and, at the population level under the paper's moment assumptions, zero characterizes independence. This gives it scope beyond monotone correlation. [Székely, Rizzo and Bakirov, 2007](https://arxiv.org/abs/0803.4101)

This is a definite engineering choice, **not a definite biological answer**. The available observations cannot establish an arbitrary high-dimensional biological mechanism. No method can guarantee sufficient power with 12–17 heterogeneous variants. A score can prioritize a relationship for measurement validation; it cannot prove an interaction, identify a causal feature, or demonstrate predictive generalization.

## What the repositories already establish

The private correlation workflow and public mirror contain 78 planned pairwise comparisons covering three outcomes and ten distinct feature annotations. Primary outcome cohorts have 17 `kcat_rel`, 16 canonical `Na_rel`, and 15 primary `v_rel` variants, with smaller complete-case feature subsets. The analysis union contains 22 variants; this is not 22 rows for every outcome. The 32 selected Morck slide/channel measurements represent five biological variants, not 32 independently exchangeable variant observations. Source: [published workflow](../../results/myh7-correlation-screen/README.md), [comparison configuration](../../results/myh7-correlation-screen/config/comparison-config-v1.json), and [decision](../myh7-correlation-screen-decision.md).

There is a concrete reason to avoid another broad predictive model search. The frozen 17-variant `kcat_rel` diagnostic already evaluated ridge and nonlinear Gaussian-process regression with nested, study-aware validation. Study-macro MAE on log outcomes was 0.25496 for the training-fold mean baseline, 0.28688 for ridge, and 0.27498 for GPR. Both failed all five registered decision rules. The public Talk-v0 diagnostic also showed no reliable predictive gain. These are repository findings, not a literature claim that ridge or GPR never work. Sources: [frozen metrics](../../results/kcat-rel/results/diagnostic-v0/metrics.json), [audit](../../results/kcat-rel/results/diagnostic-v0/AUDIT.md), and [repository status](../../README.md).

Many labels share source studies, controls, assay settings, or literal measurement components. Seven `kcat_rel`–`Na_rel` pairs reuse the same mutant short-head ATPase component; where matched WT components align, `Na_rel = L_rel / S_rel` while `kcat_rel = S_rel`. A negative association can therefore partly arise from construction. A nonlinear test cannot remove this algebra. Static WT geometry annotates sites but does not measure mutant structural shifts or conformational state populations. Source: [screen decision and dependence audit](../myh7-correlation-screen-decision.md).

## Why dependence screening fits the question

Spearman compares ranks of one variable against ranks of another. A U-shaped association can have approximately zero rho even though values are strongly related. A combined feature vector can carry information that no single feature reveals; an omnibus multivariate dependence score addresses this possibility. It does not tell us that an observed result is specifically caused by feature interactions: a strong block score can also be driven by one ordinary marginal relationship. That distinction needs targeted experiments or a predeclared predictive comparison.

The efficient implementation uses Euclidean distance matrices, double centering for the conventional score, and U-centering for the corrected score. In the U-centered construction, the squared distance-covariance numerator is unbiased under the iid model; dividing by estimated distance variances does **not** make the resulting correlation ratio an exactly unbiased estimator. The corrected squared correlation can be negative near the null and should not be square-rooted or silently clipped. Neither its negative sign nor distance correlation's unsigned magnitude describes an increasing versus decreasing biological effect. [Official dcor estimator definitions](https://dcor.readthedocs.io/en/latest/theory.html)

A large ordinary sample distance correlation is not automatically stronger evidence than a similarly sized rho. Different metrics have different sampling biases and null distributions, especially when the feature dimensions differ. High-dimensional consistency results describe what happens as data grow; they do not guarantee power in this study. Ramdas and colleagues demonstrate settings in which the power of kernel and distance tests falls as dimension increases against comparable alternatives. Prespecified compact blocks are therefore preferable to arbitrary concatenation of every annotation. [Ramdas et al., 2015](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/9692)

## Competing nonlinear dependence methods

**Multiscale Graph Correlation (MGC): strong optional sensitivity.** MGC searches smoothed neighborhood scales of distance matrices and can gain power for nonlinear geometry. Its primary biological demonstration and simulations motivate it as a plausible next method. Its superiority is empirical over the paper's evaluated settings, not universal. At this sample size, interpretation of the selected scale is particularly fragile. I would not characterize a local-scale result as proof of nonlinearity or mechanism. [Vogelstein et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.41690)

SciPy provides MGC directly, accepts multivariate inputs, and returns a statistic, permutation p-value, local map, and optimal scale. The documented five-sample minimum only says the computation can run. Its default p-value comes from permuting outcome rows, which does not automatically respect study clusters or shared controls here. If used descriptively, discard or explicitly suppress that default p-value and compare sensitivity of the statistic. [SciPy MGC documentation](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.stats.multiscale_graphcorr.html)

**HSIC: good alternative when a justified kernel encodes biology.** Hilbert–Schmidt Independence Criterion measures dependence through kernel representations and supports multivariate and structured inputs. Gaussian or other suitable characteristic kernels can detect general dependence. The original independence-test framework supplies efficient quadratic computation. It is less simple than distance correlation for this implementation because kernel and bandwidth choices become additional analysis decisions, while the present feature annotations offer no already validated biological kernel. [Gretton et al., 2007](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2007/file/d5cfead94f5350c12c322b5b664544c1-Paper.pdf)

Cluster-specific HSIC research matters, but does not provide an automatic solution for this cohort. Song, Liu and Wu explicitly model identically sampled independent clusters of equal size with the same within-cluster correlation structure. Their fast test combines within-cluster kernel information. The MYH7 literature collection has unequal group sizes, cross-label sources, heterogeneous assays and reused components; merely calling a study a cluster does not establish the published assumptions. This is a promising later inference framework if the measurement design is standardized, not a reason to report validated p-values now. [Song, Liu and Wu, 2022](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-022-26278-9)

**Mutual information: runnable but poorly matched to a primary joint screen.** Scikit-learn's estimator uses nearest-neighbor entropy estimation and reports one score per feature in natural-log units. Despite accepting an n-by-p matrix, that interface performs marginal feature screening rather than estimating the information in the full p-dimensional feature vector. Neighborhood count trades variance against bias; repeated continuous values are handled using small random noise. With many ties and few variants, these choices are consequential. This is a useful sensitivity after cohort expansion, not my default score here. [Official mutual-information estimator documentation](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.feature_selection.mutual_info_regression.html)

**High-dimensional distance-correlation t-test: do not apply its asymptotic calibration here.** Its dimension-asymptotic theorem depends on coordinate conditions and increasing dimensionality of both random vectors. Approximately ten correlated annotations versus one outcome is not the regime that justifies using its high-dimensional limit as a plug-in p-value. The bias-corrected statistic itself remains useful descriptively. [Székely and Rizzo, 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmva.2013.02.012)

## Predictive ML alternatives, ordered by suitability

**Ridge or elastic net:** ridge remains the simplest baseline if prediction becomes a new goal. Elastic net can retain correlated feature groups and performs variable selection with shrinkage; it is designed in part for p larger than n. However, both are linear in their supplied features. Pairwise nonlinear/interaction expansions of ten features create 45 interaction terms before squares, increasing the dimensional burden. Any specific interaction should be chosen from prior biology, not selected by trying all terms on these outcomes. Elastic net's grouping advantage does not establish stable biological attribution in this cohort. [Zou and Hastie, 2005](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9868.2005.00503.x)

**One-component PLS:** a defensible low-complexity linear challenger on a later prediction dataset. PLS finds projections that maximize covariance between feature and target spaces, unlike unsupervised PCA. It can handle collinearity and more features than observations. Because its projection uses the labels, it must be refit inside each training fold; computing it once on all rows before validation leaks target information. One component limits complexity but does not discover arbitrary nonlinear interactions. For three outcomes with missingness and shared components, multioutput PLS adds a smaller complete-case cohort and can model constructed cross-outcome dependence. [Official cross-decomposition guide](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/cross_decomposition.html)

**Sparse CCA:** useful for genuine two-view problems such as a large matched structural panel versus a large matched phenotyping panel. Penalized matrix decomposition regularizes high-dimensional CCA and produces sparse directions. The present three phenotypes are partially constructed from shared ATPase components, and the sample size is tiny for stable direction selection. A high in-sample canonical correlation would be particularly easy to overinterpret. Defer until standardized matched multiview data exist. [Witten, Tibshirani and Hastie, 2009](https://doi.org/10.1093/biostatistics/kxp008)

**Penalized GAM:** useful for one or two predeclared biological predictors when a smooth curve is the target. It can show interpretable nonlinear response shapes and shrink wiggliness. An additive GAM does not learn interactions unless explicit interaction smooths are added. Ten smooths against 15–17 variants leave very little data per shape. If a new mechanistic prediction question justifies it, begin with a single small-basis curve on standardized assays and validate held-out studies. Official statsmodels support includes penalized B-spline and cyclic cubic bases. [Official GAM guide](https://www.statsmodels.org/stable/gam.html)

**Kernel ridge/SVR/GPR:** capable of nonlinear multivariate response surfaces. Kernel ridge combines L2 regularization and kernel feature spaces; an RBF kernel introduces smooth nonlinear dependence without enumerating interactions. Bandwidth and regularization still require justified selection, and apparent training fit is irrelevant to generalization. Kernel ridge and GPR can have related fitted forms, but GPR supplies a probabilistic posterior rather than only minimizing squared error. GPR was already tested and failed this cohort's registered gate, so replacing it with another kernel family is not evidence-driven first priority. [Official KernelRidge API](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.kernel_ridge.KernelRidge.html), [official KRR/GPR comparison](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/auto_examples/gaussian_process/plot_compare_gpr_krr.html)

**Random forest or boosting:** nonlinear trees can represent thresholds and combinations of features. Forests average trees to reduce variance, but their default unrestricted tree sizes can overfit; meaningful constraints would include shallow depth and substantial leaf sizes. Fifteen observations do not support stable split locations across studies or confident feature importance. The fact that trees run successfully is not enough to call the result sufficient biological evidence. I would reserve a shallow forest for a materially expanded, standardized dataset with a registered prediction objective. [Official RandomForestRegressor API](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.ensemble.RandomForestRegressor.html)

## Analysis contract recommended for implementation

1. Freeze source-table hashes, block membership, primary scales, handling of constants/missingness, and output schema before calculating the new scores. State candidly that this extension is post hoc relative to the previously inspected Spearman results.
2. Retain each of the 78 historical pairwise rows. A binary-feature row should preserve the original median contrast instead of pretending it originally had rho. Calculate the new metric on the same paired variants so score differences are not caused by cohort changes.
3. Add a small number of biologically named multivariate blocks. For example, sequence chemistry uses charge and Grantham; existing site context uses catalytic proximity and RSA; new active-state geometry uses the five previously locked structural annotations. A complete-case global block can be a broad omnibus sensitivity. Do not infer that these possible groupings are biologically validated modules.
4. Standardize continuous dimensions using feature-only scale estimates before Euclidean distances. Record the complete-case variant IDs and scales. Rank-distance and natural-log-outcome alternatives can reveal whether outliers or the raw-ratio scale drive a result. These transform the estimand and must be labeled sensitivity, not selected whichever gives the largest result.
5. Report study and variant deletion ranges as sensitivity descriptions, not confidence intervals. Use outcome-specific source metadata; for outcome-versus-outcome comparisons remove the union of both source memberships when deleting a study.
6. Keep inference descriptive unless exchangeability is justified for a particular analysis. Whole-study blocks with unequal sizes are not freely interchangeable. Within-study outcome shuffling requires within-block exchangeability, including assay/control context; a study label alone does not guarantee it. Singletons supply no within-block permutations. The permutation null also changes the question to a restricted within-block association. [Winkler et al., 2015](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4644991/)
7. If prediction is added later, keep outer held-out studies untouched during all scaling, imputation, feature selection, latent projection, and tuning. Use nested study-aware validation and compare with a training-fold mean baseline. Model-selection error must be evaluated outside the tuning loop; repeated searching for an attractive cross-validation score can be optimistic even under null features. [Varma and Simon, 2006](https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2105-7-91)
8. Give each CSV row a plain 2–3 sentence description: what is compared, what the two methods summarize, and its most important limitation. Include n, dimension, variant IDs, source groups, missing reasons, binary group sizes where relevant, sensitivity ranges and source hashes. A block row's Spearman field should be blank with a reason, since a vector has no single Spearman rho.

## What would change the recommendation

A larger matched-preparation panel with uniform ATPase and velocity assays would first improve measurement comparability and permit validation of the existing rho +0.670 ATPase/velocity lead. Direct mutant-state measurements would also make the structural features more informative than static WT site geometry. With enough independent preparations/studies, the next prediction challenger would be one-component PLS or a strongly regularized model with a small predeclared feature block. A reproducible U-shaped signal in a standardized one-feature analysis would instead favor a tightly constrained GAM. Evidence for genuine multifeature interaction would justify a registered kernel model comparison. At present, the principal next scientific action remains matched measurement validation, supported by a richer dependence screen rather than replaced by it.
